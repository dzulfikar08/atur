Asana is the safer choice for most teams, and it is worth starting there. It is mature, the workflow builder is genuinely good, and a team whose need is "track any kind of work with structure" will be well served. Atur competes from a different corner: it is one workspace built around the pieces of a small operation — issues, support tickets, deals, time, pages — with AI agents that act on all of them through the same door. The honest comparison is breadth-of-platform versus coherence-of-workspace.

## Where Asana wins

Asana's strengths are real and specific. The automation builder (when-this-then-that rules on any project) is more capable than anything Atur ships. Portfolios and goal tracking give larger orgs a management layer Atur deliberately does not have. Forms turn requests into structured intake. And the app ecosystem is enormous: if your stack is already Asana-shaped, staying is cheap.

A team of five sub-teams that reports upward — marketing, ops, product, each with their own project shapes — is Asana's home turf. Atur's flat projects-and-states model would feel constraining there, and saying so is more useful than pretending otherwise.

## The assembled-workspace tax

The pattern Atur is built against: Asana for work, a helpdesk for mail, a CRM for deals, a timesheet tool for hours, a wiki for docs. Each is fine; the seams are not. The support ticket that needs a linked issue, the deal that needs delivery status, the hours that need to land on the right project — each seam is a copy-paste, a Zapier rule, or a meeting where someone reads from two tabs.

In Atur those are links in one database. A ticket escalates into an issue and keeps its history; a deal's renewal call shows the tickets, the features, and the hours behind it; the timesheet is a view over work, not a separate place to reconstruct it. The trade-off is the same one as [Linear][l] and [ClickUp][c]: no single surface is as deep as the specialist, and the win only counts if your week genuinely crosses the seams.

## Where agents change the comparison

Asana has AI features — summaries, smart status. They read your work and write prose about it. Atur's bet is agents that do the work through MCP: 70+ tools that create issues, move tickets through the same states a human would, log time, and update deals, with API keys scoped per project so an agent's reach is bounded by design.

The difference is direction. An AI summary is one more tab to read; an agent that triages the inbox into filed tickets and drafts the replies every morning removes a task. Teams adopting agent workflows care about that difference, and it is the reason Atur exists.

## Pricing, plainly

Atur is $9/month Starter, $29/month Team, full workspace on trial, no credit card. Asana's per-seat pricing rises with the tier that unlocks the feature you actually wanted — portfolios and advanced automation live above the starter price — and at small-team scale the two come out similar; at larger seat counts Asana costs more.
