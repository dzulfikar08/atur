Linear is a better issue tracker than Atur, and it is worth saying so up front. For teams whose work is almost entirely issues, cycles, and roadmaps, Linear's speed, polish, and keyboard-first flow are hard to beat. Atur is not trying to be the sharpest tool for that job. It is trying to be the only tool a small operation needs, even when the work is not just engineering.

## Conceding the tracker

Linear's advantage is in its constraints. The interface stays fast because it does fewer things; the cycle model is clean because it does not also model deals, tickets, and time entries. Teams that live inside a single function (product and engineering, mostly) will find Linear quicker to use and easier to learn.

If your backlog is your biggest problem and your support mail goes somewhere else, Linear is the more honest recommendation.

## Where the rest of the operation lives

The case for Atur is not that its issue tracker is better, but that work does not stop at the issue. The same team that uses boards likely also answers customer mail, manages a small number of deals, logs a few billable hours, and keeps a page or two of notes. In Linear, each of those is a different tool with a different login and no shared history.

Atur collapses them into one workspace. A support ticket links to the issue that fixes it. A deal links to the features the customer asked for. Time logged on an issue lands on the right project automatically. A page about a feature links forward to the open issues blocking it.

A concrete case: a SaaS team that tracks engineering in Linear and support in a shared inbox has no single view of whether a promised feature is in flight. In Atur, the proposal page links to the issue that satisfies it, the support ticket that surfaced it, and the time logged against both, so the renewal call can be honest about delivery without hunting across tools. That connection is what most small teams need most days.

That is a trade-off, not a universal win. The price of integration is that no single piece is as fast as Linear for pure issue tracking. But for teams whose work spans the border between issues and everything that surrounds them, the cost of context-switching between best-in-class pieces is higher than the cost of a less-polished tracker.

## When Atur is the wrong choice

Atur is the wrong choice for a product or engineering team that only needs issue tracking. If your support inbox, CRM, and timesheets are already solved (by other teams, other vendors, or genuine disinterest), then the broadness that Atur offers becomes noise, and Linear stays the better call.

## Pricing, plainly

Atur's web workspace is free. Solo ($9/month) and Team ($29/month, for everyone in the workspaces you own) add the phone app. Linear's pricing is per editor, with the final cost depending on seat count and optional add-ons; see their site for the current number.
