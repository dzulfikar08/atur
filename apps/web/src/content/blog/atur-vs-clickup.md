ClickUp sells the same promise Atur does (one subscription instead of five) and it has the widest feature checklist in the category: tasks, docs, goals, whiteboards, a CRM layer, time tracking, and AI. But there is a difference between bundling apps and connecting work. ClickUp's breadth comes from templates and views over a generic task store; Atur's comes from a data model where a ticket, a deal, an issue, and a page are the same graph.

## Where ClickUp wins

If your team wants maximum flexibility and is comfortable configuring it, ClickUp's surface area is genuinely enormous. Templates cover sales pipelines, marketing calendars, and engineering boards; views let the same data look like a list, a board, or a timeline; and the ecosystem of integrations is mature. For teams that treat the tool as a configurable shell and enjoy the setup, it is a capable choice.

## Atur's case

The difference shows up the moment work crosses a domain. In ClickUp, a "CRM" is a pipeline built from a template: a list view with stages, on top of the same task object as everything else. There is no structural connection between a customer record and a support ticket or a time entry; the link is whatever someone remembered to put in a custom field.

Atur does not bolt a CRM on. Contacts, companies, and deals live as first-class records, and a deal can open a support ticket, surface the issues keeping it alive, and accumulate time entries. That is not a template. It is how the data model works by default. The support portal has real SLA timers and turns inbound mail into tracked tickets; time entries land against issues and roll up into one-click CSV exports; pages link to the work they describe with backlinks and a knowledge graph.

For a small team, the practical difference is the maintenance tax. A ClickUp stack needs someone to own the templates, keep custom fields consistent, and re-check the automations whenever a view drifts. Atur's connected default means the connections exist before anyone configures them: the context is there on day one, not after a setup project.

## When Atur is the wrong choice

If you genuinely need ClickUp's breadth (say, a team that lives in deeply custom views and needs an open-ended builder), the template approach may fit better. Atur is for small teams that want the operating pieces connected out of the box, without making someone the tool's part-time administrator.

## Pricing, plainly

Atur's web workspace is free. The paid plans only add the phone app: Solo is $9/month for one person, Team is a flat $29/month for everyone in the workspaces you own, not a per-seat charge. ClickUp prices per member per month, and the cost grows with every seat and add-on; see their site for current numbers.
